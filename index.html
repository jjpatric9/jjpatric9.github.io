BoxStacker

A stacking game about balance. A crane holds the crate you picked from your hand; you slide it sideways and drop it. Complete a row and it sets, becoming the tower's new foundation — but the crates you're dealt rarely let you finish a row cleanly, and every hole and overhang pushes the tower further off centre. Lean too far and the whole thing goes over.

How these games are built

Six years of software development, now channelled into games. I build with AI tooling, and I care most about the things players feel but never see:

  • Input that never fights you. The piece hangs from the crane and only ever moves sideways — a whole axis of awkwardness removed rather than worked around.
  • Difficulty that's measured, not guessed. Each hand is generated against the board actually in front of you, then played out by bots hundreds of times to check it's survivable before you're dealt it.
  • Rules that are testable. The whole ruleset lives in a module with no Android dependencies, so thousands of complete games are played against it before a build ships.
  • Nothing you build leaves the phone. There is no account, no sign-in and no server of mine — your record, your Brag Book and the run in progress are all local, and no code path sends any of it anywhere. The app does use the network, for one thing only: it is free and adverts pay for it, so Google AdMob is in there and the privacy policy says exactly what that collects.
More games are coming, in the same shape: small, offline, and respectful of your attention.
